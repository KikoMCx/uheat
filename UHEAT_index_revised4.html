Understanding ocean heat content and its role in marine heatwaves and ecosystem change

A joint European–Japanese research project investigating how ocean heat redistribution drives marine heatwaves and impacts on fisheries.

2026–2029 · Atlantic & Pacific Oceans · EIG CONCERT-Japan

About the project

Understanding how ocean heat accumulates and moves around the planet, and how these changes shape marine heatwaves and their impacts on ecosystems.

Around 90% of the excess heat trapped by global warming has been absorbed by the ocean. However, this heat is not distributed uniformly. Ocean circulation redistributes heat across the globe and transports it into the ocean interior, shaping regional patterns of ocean warming.

In some regions, changes in ocean circulation are accelerating warming, creating conditions that favour increasingly frequent and intense marine heatwaves (MHWs).

Marine heatwaves can have profound consequences for marine ecosystems, affecting biodiversity, fisheries, and the communities that depend on healthy oceans.

UHEAT brings together researchers from Spain, Japan, and Germany to understand how changes in ocean heat content (OHC) influence MHWs and their impact on key commercially important fish species. By combining observations, climate models, and artificial intelligence, the project aims to advance understanding of regional OHC changes and their connection to MHWs, and exploit this understanding to develop AI-based models for the prediction of MHWs and their impacts on key fish species.

Research objectives and approach

From reconstructing past ocean changes to anticipating future impacts.

01

Estimating past changes

Reconstruct ocean heat content, heat transport, and circulation across the Atlantic and Pacific since 1993, using a rigorous Bayesian method that fuses satellite and in-situ ocean data.

02

Explaining the changes

Determine what's actually causing regional ocean heat changes — and how those changes precondition marine heatwaves.

03

Projecting the future

Use "emergent constraints" to refine climate model projections of future ocean heat content and marine heatwave likelihood.

04

Predicting marine heatwaves with AI

Build a deep-learning system that predicts marine heatwave probability using ocean heat content, heat transport, and sea surface temperature — going beyond today's SST-only AI models.

05

Assessing the impact on fisheries

Translate heatwave predictions into practical, ecosystem-level insight: how will key commercial fish species' habitats shift, and what does that mean for fisheries management in Japan and Europe?
